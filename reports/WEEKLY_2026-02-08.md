- [**sCellST predicts single-cell gene expression from H&E images**](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-025-67965-1) — 2026-01-09, *Nature Communications*

  `gene expression prediction` `single-cell resolution` `deep learning`

  Introduces a deep learning model that predicts single-cell gene expression directly from H&E morphology, moving beyond the small image patches used by existing approaches to capture fine-grained morphological variation at single-cell resolution. The model matches patch-based methods on spot-level prediction tasks while recovering biologically meaningful expression patterns across two cancer datasets and distinguishing fine cell populations, enabling molecular-level interpretation of standard histological slides at scale without the need for costly spatial transcriptomics experiments.

- [**SPEED: Denoising spatial epigenomic data via deep matrix factorization**](https://doi.org/10.1038/s43588-025-00941-3) — 2026-01-13, *Nature Computational Science*

  `spatial epigenomics` `denoising` `imputation` `deep matrix factorization`

  Addresses the critical challenge of low signal detection and extreme sparsity in spatial epigenomics (SE) data. SPEED is a deep matrix factorization framework that leverages atlas-level single-cell epigenomic data together with spatial context to impute and denoise SE data. In comprehensive benchmarks on both simulated and real tissue datasets, SPEED outperformed five state-of-the-art methods across diverse tissues and technologies. The denoised outputs facilitate downstream analyses such as differential chromatin accessibility analysis, epigenomic spatial domain identification, and gene activity inference.

- [**DBiTplus: Integration of imaging-based and sequencing-based spatial omics mapping on the same tissue section**](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41592-025-02948-0) — 2026-01-15, *Nature Methods*

  `multi-modal spatial omics` `data integration` `single-cell resolution` `spatial barcoding`

  Presents Deterministic Barcoding in Tissue sequencing plus (DBiTplus), a unified multimodal spatial omics approach that combines sequencing-based spatial transcriptomics and multiplexed protein imaging on the same tissue section. Using spatial barcoding and RNase H-mediated cDNA retrieval, DBiTplus preserves tissue architecture for multiplexed protein imaging while enabling transcriptome-wide interrogation. The computational pipelines developed for integration allow imaging-guided deconvolution to generate single-cell-resolved spatial transcriptome atlases. Demonstrated across frozen mouse embryos and FFPE human lymphoma tissues, uncovering mechanisms of lymphomagenesis, progression, and transformation.

- [**Impact and correction of segmentation errors in spatial transcriptomics**](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41588-025-02497-4) — 2026-01-20, *Nature Genetics*

  `cell segmentation` `error correction` `imaging-based spatial transcriptomics` `quality control`

  Reveals that segmentation errors in imaging-based spatial transcriptomics currently confound most downstream analyses of cellular state, including differential expression, neighbor influence, and ligand-receptor interactions — with misassigned molecules frequently dominating results. Re-analysis of data from multiple tissues and platforms shows that the extent to which misassigned molecules impact results can be striking. The authors show that matrix factorization of local molecular neighborhoods can effectively identify and isolate molecular admixtures, reducing their impact on downstream analyses in a manner analogous to doublet filtering in scRNA-seq.

- [**PathCLAST: pathway-augmented contrastive learning with attention for interpretable spatial transcriptomics**](https://doi.org/10.1093/bib/bbag029) — 2026-01, *Briefings in Bioinformatics*

  `spatial domain identification` `contrastive learning` `pathway analysis` `interpretability`

  Integrates gene expression, histopathological images, and curated pathway graphs via bi-modal contrastive learning for spatial domain identification. By embedding expression profiles into biologically structured graphs and aligning them with local image features, PathCLAST achieves state-of-the-art spatial domain identification across four benchmark datasets (37% improvement over STAGATE on the IDC dataset), outperforming eleven competing methods. Uniquely provides pathway-level attention scores for mechanistic interpretation, uncovering domain-specific pathways, spatially organized signaling, and intra-domain heterogeneity relevant to tumor progression.

- [**SpaCo: spectral dimension reduction for spatial transcriptomics**](https://doi.org/10.1093/bioinformatics/btag052) — 2026-01-31, *Bioinformatics*

  `dimension reduction` `spatially variable genes` `spatial pattern detection` `Moran's I`

  Introduces a statistical approach that projects multivariate spatial transcriptomics data onto a low-dimensional feature space optimal for maximizing Moran's I, a measure of spatial dependency. This projection mitigates non-spatial variation and significantly outperforms PCA for preprocessing. Patterns of spatially variable genes are well represented in the projected space, and the projection acts as a denoising operation. The framework is parameter-free and gives rise to a calibrated, powerful test of spatial gene expression. Available as an R package.

- [**ST-LDAW: Topic-Model and Damped Weighted Least-Squares Method for Integrative Deconvolution of Spatial Transcriptomics**](https://doi.org/10.21203/rs.3.rs-8566542/v1) — 2026-01-13, *bioRxiv*

  `cell-type deconvolution` `topic modeling` `robust estimation` `rare cell types`

  Combines probabilistic topic modeling with damped weighted least-squares optimization to address the instability problem in spot-level cell-type deconvolution. Topic-based modeling reduces dimensionality and mitigates gene-level noise by capturing coherent transcriptional programs, while damped weighting constrains the influence of unstable features, preventing variance inflation and overfitting. Achieves 94% recall and 80% accuracy on simulated spatial mixtures, surpassing existing regression-based and mapping-based methods. Application to breast cancer ST data reveals subtype-specific cellular composition and intercellular communication patterns.

- [**Emerge: Microenvironment-aware transcriptome reconstruction in spatial transcriptomics**](https://doi.org/10.21203/rs.3.rs-8566824/v1) — 2026-01-13, *bioRxiv*

  `transcriptome reconstruction` `imputation` `optimal transport` `microenvironment`

  Addresses the limitation that imaging-based spatial transcriptomics measures limited gene panels dominated by identity-defining genes, leaving transcriptome-wide variation unobserved. Emerge reconstructs transcriptome-scale expression by jointly modeling intrinsic transcriptional manifolds from scRNA-seq and extrinsic niche organization from spatial data within a type-constrained optimal transport formulation. Across fourteen MERFISH and Xenium datasets from neural and tumor tissues, Emerge improves prediction accuracy, spatial coherence, and recovery of within-type heterogeneity. Reveals microenvironment-stratified astrocyte, stromal, and fibroblast states only partially captured by existing approaches.

  `gene expression prediction` `pan-cancer` `flow matching` `mixture of experts` `generative model`

  Introduces a generative model for pan-cancer histogenomic prediction that addresses the fragmentation of single-tissue models. MoLF uses a conditional Flow Matching objective to map noise to the gene latent manifold, parameterized by a Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) velocity field that dynamically routes inputs to specialized sub-networks, effectively decoupling the optimization of diverse tissue patterns. Notably exhibits zero-shot generalization to cross-species data, suggesting it captures fundamental, conserved histo-molecular mechanisms.

- [**hSNMF: Hybrid Spatially Regularized NMF for Image-Derived Spatial Transcriptomics**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.02638v1) — 2026-02-02, *arXiv*

  `spatial domain detection` `clustering` `non-negative matrix factorization` `Xenium`

  Benchmarks and extends NMF for high-resolution spatial transcriptomics by introducing two spatially regularized variants for Xenium data. Spatial NMF (SNMF) enforces local spatial smoothness by diffusing NMF factor vectors over spatial neighborhoods. Hybrid Spatial NMF (hSNMF) combines spatially regularized NMF with Leiden clustering on a hybrid adjacency integrating spatial proximity and transcriptomic similarity via a tunable mixing parameter. Evaluated on cholangiocarcinoma, both methods achieve markedly improved spatial compactness (CHAOS < 0.004, Moran's I > 0.96), greater cluster separability (Silhouette > 0.12), and higher biological coherence compared to baselines.

- [**ImmuVis: Hyperconvolutional Foundation Model for Imaging Mass Cytometry**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.04585v1) — 2026-02-04, *arXiv*

  `foundation model` `imaging mass cytometry` `virtual staining` `self-supervised learning`

  Presents an efficient convolutional foundation model for imaging mass cytometry (IMC) that handles the unique challenge of variable channel spaces across studies. ImmuVis introduces marker-adaptive hyperconvolutions that generate convolutional kernels from learned marker embeddings, enabling a single model to operate on arbitrary measured marker subsets without retraining. Pretrained on IMC17M — the largest IMC dataset to date (28 cohorts, 24,405 images, 265 markers, 17M+ patches) — using self-supervised masked reconstruction. Outperforms SOTA baselines in virtual staining and downstream classification at substantially lower compute cost than transformer-based alternatives, and is the sole model providing calibrated uncertainty via a heteroscedastic likelihood objective.
